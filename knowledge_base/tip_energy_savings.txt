GENERAL ENERGY-SAVING TIPS

Room temperature: The German Environment Agency (UBA) recommends about 20 degrees Celsius in living rooms, 18 degrees in the kitchen and 17 degrees in bedrooms. Each degree less saves around 6% of heating energy.

Ventilation: Open windows fully for 5-10 minutes several times a day (shock ventilation) instead of leaving them tilted. Tilted windows cool the walls and waste heat for hours. Turn thermostats down while ventilating.

Standby consumption: Televisions, routers, consoles, coffee machines and chargers draw power around the clock. Standby can make up 5-10% of a household's electricity use. Switchable power strips or smart plugs cut it off at night.

Lighting: LED bulbs use about 80% less electricity than incandescent bulbs and last much longer.

Hot water: A water-saving shower head (about 6-9 litres per minute) cuts hot water use noticeably. With a heat pump, keep the hot water temperature moderate (around 48-50 degrees Celsius) and let the installer configure a regular legionella protection cycle.

Insulation and drafts: Seal gaps around windows and doors, close roller shutters at night in winter, and keep radiators free of furniture and curtains so heat can circulate.

Know your consumption: A typical German household of three to four people uses about 3,500-4,500 kWh of electricity per year without heat pump and electric car. A heat pump adds roughly 3,000-5,000 kWh, an electric car about 2,000-2,500 kWh for 12,000 km. Comparing your own figures with these values shows where the big savings are.

Price awareness: With a dynamic tariff the same kWh can cost much more in the evening than at midday. Shifting flexible consumption is often worth more than small reductions.

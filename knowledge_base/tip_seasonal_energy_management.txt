SEASONAL ENERGY MANAGEMENT

Energy needs change through the year. Adjusting habits by season avoids waste and aligns usage with solar production and prices.

Summer: Cooling dominates the bill. Keep the thermostat at 78F when home, use ceiling fans, close blinds on sun-facing windows, and run heat-producing appliances (oven, dryer) in the early morning or evening. Solar production is at its peak, with long days from about 06:00 to 20:00, so use midday for pre-cooling and EV charging. Peak electricity prices usually fall between 17:00 and 21:00 - raise the setpoint by 2-4F during that window.

Winter: Heating dominates. Set the thermostat to 68F when home and 60-62F when away. Production from solar drops by 50-70% and days are short (about 09:00-16:00), so expect more grid imports. Open south-facing blinds during the day for free heat and close them at night. Seal drafts around doors and windows, which can cut heating loss by 10-20%. Pre-heat in the late afternoon before the evening peak.

Spring and autumn: Mild weather means HVAC can often stay off. Use natural ventilation (open windows at night, close in the day). This is the best time for maintenance - service the heat pump or furnace, clean filters, and check insulation before the extreme season arrives. Use free solar surplus to charge batteries and EVs.

Holidays and vacations: Lower the thermostat, unplug standby loads, set the water heater to vacation mode and keep only the refrigerator running.

Daylight and lighting: Lighting demand rises in winter evenings; use LEDs and timers. In summer, use natural light as much as possible.

Water heating: Set the tank to 120F (49C) all year; in summer, shift heating to solar hours. Winter hot water demand rises, so insulate the tank and pipes.

Pool pumps: Run a pool pump 4-6 hours in the middle of the day in summer when solar is abundant and in the early afternoon in cooler months; avoid peak hours. In winter, reduce runtime to 2-4 hours if water temperatures are low.

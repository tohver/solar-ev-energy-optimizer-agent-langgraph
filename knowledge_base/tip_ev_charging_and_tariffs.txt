EV CHARGING, DYNAMIC TARIFFS AND GRID RULES IN GERMANY

The electric car is usually the largest flexible load in the home. A compact EV needs about 15-20 kWh per 100 km. An 11 kW wallbox adds about 60 km of range per hour of charging, so a typical daily commute is recharged in about one hour.

Dynamic tariffs: Since 2025 every electricity supplier in Germany must offer a dynamic tariff to customers with a smart meter (section 41a of the Energy Industry Act, EnWG). The price follows the day-ahead market of the power exchange (now in 15-minute steps), plus fixed components: grid fees, levies, electricity tax, concession fee, supplier margin and 19% VAT. The fixed components are a large part of the final price, so household price swings are smaller than on the exchange, but a spread of 10-20 cents per kWh within one day is common.

When prices are known: The day-ahead auction for the next day closes at 12:00; results are published around 12:45-13:00. Before that, prices for tomorrow can only be estimated.

Cheapest times to charge: Usually at night and, on sunny days, around midday when solar power floods the market. The evening (about 17:00-20:00) is usually the most expensive time. Negative exchange prices occur on sunny, windy days with low demand; with a dynamic tariff the household price then drops accordingly but usually stays above zero because of the fixed components.

Charging from solar: Charging from your own PV system costs only the feed-in tariff you give up (around 8 cents per kWh), far less than grid power. Use a wallbox with a solar surplus mode, or set the charging window from late morning until mid-afternoon on sunny days.

Section 14a EnWG: Wallboxes above 4.2 kW installed since 2024 count as controllable consumer devices. The grid operator may temporarily limit their power to 4.2 kW when the local grid is overloaded; it never switches them off completely. In return the household gets reduced grid fees: a flat annual reduction (module 1), a reduced price for separately metered devices (module 2), or since 2025 additionally time-variable grid fees (module 3) that are cheaper in off-peak hours.

Registration: An 11 kW wallbox must be reported to the grid operator; a 22 kW wallbox needs approval.

Battery care: For daily use charge to about 80% and charge to 100% only before long trips. In winter, pre-heat the car while it is plugged in; cold reduces the range by about 15-30%.
